# Big Picture Tab — Build Process

The Big Picture tab is the diagnostic and directional foundation of the PLAN workbook. It maps the current state, the tension, the destination, and the non-negotiable conditions across every functional area of the business.

It is not a strategy document. It is not an action plan. It is the honest picture of where things stand and where things need to get to — the working behind the strategy.

---

## What It Is Not

- Not prose. Every cell is a short phrase or two — maximum.
- Not a solution. Expectation states the outcome. Requirement states the condition. Neither names the how.
- Not granular. If you are naming a solution, you have gone too far. Execution detail belongs in the Timeline and Support workbooks.
- Not an audit. Complications name the real tension — they do not indict the business.

---

## Tab Structure

**Rows (fixed):**
- **Situation** — Facts only. Where things stand today.
- **Complication** — What makes the situation insufficient or unsustainable. Names the tension, not a list of problems.
- **Expectation** — Where it needs to get to. Opportunity-forward — not a negation of the Complication. State the outcome, not the action.
- **Requirement** — What must be true for the expectation to be met. Non-negotiable conditions only.

**Columns (standard template — adapt per engagement):**
Market | Brand | Marketing | Distribution | Service | Logistics | Supply Chain | Organization

## Build Process

### Phase 1 — Read Everything

Before forming any view or touching any column, read every available document in the Project:
- Brand and consumer strategy document
- Consumer universe and persona files
- All journey maps (every available persona)
- Briefs, diagnostic documents, analytics reports
- Constitution, manifestos, brand blueprint
- Product catalog or any operational reference material

**This is mandatory, not optional.** What is already decided does not get reconstructed. Extract before you invent. If the client's own framing exists in a document, use it — do not default to assumptions.

### Phase 2 — External Research

A strategy built only on internal documents is incomplete. After reading all internal material, research the external environment:
- Market size, growth trends, category dynamics
- Consumer behavior shifts relevant to the business
- Competitive landscape — who exists, what they do, where the gaps are
- Geographic or regulatory context where relevant

External research is required, not supplementary. It informs every column where it is relevant, not just Market. All figures used must come from live web searches — never training-data figures. Source URLs are listed below the table in the Excel file, not in cells.

### Phase 3 — CEO Briefing

After reading all documents and completing external research, request a focused briefing from Michele before touching any column.

The purpose is to surface what documents cannot answer:
- Her strategic read on the current state
- Her instincts on where the business needs to go
- Any context only she holds as the person closest to the client and the engagement
- Any constraints, sensitivities, or recent developments not yet documented

**Get this once, at the start.** Do not extract it piecemeal column by column. The briefing should get the work close enough to a final draft that column-by-column input is minimal thereafter.

### Phase 4 — Draft All Columns

With full context in hand, draft all columns before presenting anything to Michele.

**Column-by-column is a hard rule during the build** — present one column at a time for reaction, do not output multiple columns simultaneously unless Michele explicitly requests it.

For each column, arrive with:
- A point of view on all four rows, grounded in the source material and research
- The judgment already applied — what matters, what does not
- Any single question that is genuinely blocking progress and only Michele can answer

### Phase 5 — Review and Lock

Present one column at a time. Michele reacts. You adjust.

**Feedback is surgical.** When Michele gives input on specific areas, address only those areas. Do not re-output the full column or full document. Changes are targeted and precise.

Lock each column before moving to the next. A locked column does not reopen unless Michele explicitly requests it.

### Phase 6 — Debrief

After all columns are locked, before moving to the next tab:
- Summarize the through-line: what single story does the Big Picture tell?
- Connect it back to the existing strategy document if one exists
- Confirm the story is consistent and Michele agrees before proceeding

**The debrief is a hard gate.** Do not move to Customer Objectives or any subsequent tab until it is complete and confirmed.

## Column-by-Column Guidance

**Market**
What is happening in the external environment — consumer trends, market dynamics, competitive landscape, regulatory or economic context. Always requires live research. Cite sources.

**Brand**
Current state of brand identity, recognition, and transferability. Is the brand documented? Is it dependent on one person? Where does it need to be known and by whom? Use the client's own brand documents as the primary source — do not reconstruct what is already defined.

**Marketing**
What marketing exists today and what is its nature — intentional or de facto? What channels, owned assets, infrastructure exist? What is missing? Read journey maps before making any channel categorization decisions — affiliate, partnership, collaboration, and distribution channels have specific definitions in the journey architecture that must be respected.

**Distribution**
How does product currently reach customers? Which channels exist and are they fit for the target customer, in the right sequence? Distribution is about where and how product changes hands — not how awareness is created. Persona sequencing determines channel priority and timing — not all channels serve all personas at once.

**Service**
Who delivers service and how? Is it scalable? Is the knowledge behind it documented? What does the brand promise, and can it be kept at volume? Expectation should be opportunity-forward — frame what great looks like, not just what is currently broken.

**Logistics**
How does physical product move? What are the friction points — cost, condition, geography, personal dependency? Think beyond shipping: physical reach, events, and alternative fulfilment models are all logistics questions. What needs to change for the model to scale sustainably?

**Supply Chain**
Where does product come from? Is provenance documented and defensible? What are the production planning constraints and lead times? Where is the integrity risk as volume grows?

**Organization**
Who does what? Where is the founder's time going? What is the organizational ceiling? Expectation should frame the opportunity — what the founder's role becomes, not just what it currently costs. What needs to exist internally or externally before the next phase activates?

## Register Rules

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Situation | Facts only. No judgment. |
| Complication | Names the tension. Does not catastrophize. Does not indict. |
| Expectation | Opportunity-forward. Not a negation of the Complication. Outcome, not action. |
| Requirement | Non-negotiable conditions only. Does not name the solution. |
| All cells | Short phrases. Never prose paragraphs. |
| Execution detail | Never. If you are naming a solution or a specific mechanism, move it to Timeline. |

---

## Writing Into Excel

When writing confirmed content into the Excel file:
- Text only — no formatting changes, no color, no merged cells, no structural modifications
- Match the client's existing file format exactly — verify layout cell by cell before writing
- Write into the correct cells only
- Never overwrite existing client content without explicit instruction
- Save as a new version (v02, v03, etc.) — never overwrite the source file

---

## Output

Completed Big Picture tab written into the client's PLAN workbook Excel file — text only, client formatting preserved, new version saved, debrief completed, through-line confirmed.
