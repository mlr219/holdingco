# Brand Objectives Tab — Build Process

The Brand Objectives tab defines what brand success looks like in measurable terms over the plan horizon. It answers the question: how do we know the brand is becoming what it needs to become? It is not a marketing plan. It is not a list of activities. It is a set of hard, trackable indicators that prove the brand is building real equity with the right audience.

---

## What It Is Not

- Not qualitative. No surveys, no focus groups, no subjective scoring. Every KPI must be trackable from a real data source — analytics, search tools, a manual log, or a verifiable count.
- Not a standard brand health dashboard. Generic KPIs (follower counts, impressions, NPS) are rarely the right measures for a specialist niche brand. The KPIs must be derived from the brand's specific goals, not imported from a template.
- Not independent. Brand Objectives must be consistent with the Customer Objectives already set — atelier % of revenue derives from the customer revenue model; B2B inbound leads must be consistent with B2B revenue targets in Top Line.
- Not activity-based. Measures reflect outcomes, not effort. "Number of press releases sent" is not a KPI. "Earned media placements in relevant publications" is.

---

## Tab Structure

KPIs grouped by strategic objective — not a flat list. Each group maps to a specific brand expectation from the Big Picture tab.

The number of KPIs is not fixed. Use as many measures as the brand's strategic objectives require — grouped logically, each earning its place by connecting to a specific expectation. Do not default to five measures because that is a common template convention.

Each KPI row contains:
- The measure name and data source
- Current baseline
- Annual targets across the plan horizon (current year through final plan year)

All figures are annual. No mixing of monthly and annual units within the same table. Where the current baseline is not yet known, mark as TBC and flag for client confirmation — targets set without a confirmed baseline are directional only.

## Build Process

### Phase 1 — Read the Brand Column of the Big Picture

Before defining any KPI, read what was locked in the Brand column:
- Situation: what does the brand look like today?
- Expectation: what does the brand need to become?
- Requirement: what must be true for that to happen?

The KPIs must prove the Expectation is being met. If a KPI does not connect to the Expectation, it does not belong in this tab.

### Phase 2 — Define the Strategic Objectives the KPIs Must Prove

Translate the brand Expectation into 2-4 strategic objectives — the specific things the brand needs to demonstrate over the plan horizon. These become the groups in the tab.

Examples of well-formed strategic objectives:
- The brand exists as a named, recognizable entity (independent of founder presence)
- The brand is reaching the right audience in the right circles
- The brand is being sought out — pull, not push

Each client engagement will produce different objectives. Derive them from the Big Picture, not from a template.

### Phase 3 — Research the Right KPIs for This Brand at This Stage

Before proposing any KPI, research what the right measures are for:
- This type of brand (niche specialist, founder-led, early-stage, B2B+B2C hybrid, etc.)
- This stage of development (building from zero vs. scaling vs. defending)
- This category and audience (what signals indicate brand equity in this specific market)

Early-stage niche brands require different measures than mature brands. Branded search volume and inbound requests are more meaningful than reach or impressions at this stage. Research validates KPI selection — do not default to the most commonly cited brand metrics without checking whether they are the right ones for this moment.

Hard measures to consider by objective type:
- **Brand existence/recognition:** branded search volume, direct traffic %, atelier/premium % of total revenue
- **Audience reach:** earned media placements in relevant publications, referral traffic volume, backlinks from credible relevant domains
- **Brand pull:** inbound partnership and collaboration requests, inbound press and media requests, B2B inbound leads (customer-initiated, not outbound)
- **Engagement depth:** return visitor rate, pages per session, content-driven traffic as % of total

### Phase 4 — Propose the KPI Set and Get Alignment

Present the proposed KPI groups and measures to Michele before setting any targets. Confirm:
- Do the groups map correctly to the brand expectations?
- Are all measures genuinely hard and trackable?
- Is anything missing that the brand's specific situation requires?
- Are there measures in the existing tab that should be replaced or reframed?

Do not set targets until the KPI set is confirmed. Targets against the wrong measures are wasted effort.

### Phase 5 — Establish Current Baselines

For each confirmed KPI, establish the current baseline:
- Pull from available analytics (GA4, Search Console, existing reports)
- Pull from client knowledge (inbound request counts, earned media count)
- Where the client must confirm, mark TBC — do not estimate baselines, only targets

Flag all TBC baselines as open questions for the client. These must be resolved before the tab is considered final.

### Phase 6 — Set Annual Targets

For each KPI, set targets year by year across the plan horizon:
- Align the growth curve with the customer sequencing — brand metrics lag marketing activity by 6-12 months; account for this in the trajectory
- Be honest about where the brand starts — a brand starting from near-zero cannot reach mature brand metrics in year one
- Targets should compound — early years build the foundation, middle years show acceleration, later years show steady state
- Annual figures only — no monthly figures in the table

### Phase 7 — Cross-Tab Triangulation Checks

Before locking, verify consistency across tabs:
- Atelier % of revenue must match the customer revenue model in Customer Objectives
- B2B inbound leads must be consistent with B2B revenue targets that will be set in Top Line
- Brand awareness trajectory must be plausible given the marketing investment implied by the customer sequencing

Flag any inconsistencies as open questions before writing to the workbook.

### Phase 8 — Flag Open Questions

Log any TBC baselines and any assumptions requiring client confirmation directly in the sheet below the data table. Label clearly.

### Phase 9 — Present Once, Complete

Do not present the tab until:
- KPI groups and measures are confirmed
- All available baselines are populated (TBC where genuinely unknown)
- All annual targets are set
- Cross-tab checks are complete
- Open questions are logged in the sheet

## Excel Writing Rules

- Group KPIs visually by strategic objective — label each group clearly
- Annual figures throughout — no monthly units
- Percentages as decimals or % — be consistent within the tab
- TBC where baseline is genuinely unknown — never estimate a baseline
- Open questions logged as plain text below the data table
- Add 2031 column if Customer Objectives runs to 2031 — tabs must be consistent
- No formatting changes beyond what is explicitly requested
- Save as new version — never overwrite the source file

---

## Reconciliation Note

Brand Objectives feed into the Top Line reconciliation. When Top Line is built, verify that the implied marketing investment and channel activity is consistent with the brand metric targets set here. If the brand targets require more reach or earned media than the marketing plan can generate, either the targets or the plan must be adjusted. Reconciliation is a Top Line step — do not attempt it here.
